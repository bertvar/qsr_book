Overview of the module

  • Develop fundamental data analytical skills necessary to conduct quantitative social research:

    • exploratory data analysis
    • statistical modelling

  • Eminently applied and organised around hands-on work with widely used datasets in sociology, social policy, criminology and the wider social sciences.

  • Aimed at students wanting to develop the key skills to conduct applied quantitative research in academic - especially UG dissertations - and industry settings.

Learning outcomes

  • Demonstrate understanding and command of standard tools for quantitative social research

  • Identify the appropriate methods to analyse different types of data and evaluate their strengths and limitations in both their own analysis and published research.

  • Evidence their ability to prepare, analyse and interpret quantitative data to answer substantive research questions using statistical software.

  • Effectively present and report findings from quantitative data analysis using graphs and tables in a clear and systematic way to meet the needs of different types of audiences.

What does it take to do QSR?

Assessment I (10% of final mark)

  • 7 x Weekly take-home exercises ( 4 x 1% + 3 x 2% = 10% final mark)

  • This is the main mechanism for formative feedback

  • Practice at home the techniques covered in the workshops.

  • Crucial to keep engaged throughout the semester and ensure a good grasp of module material.

  • It will be released simultaneously with the relevant unit and will be due on the Wednesday the week after.

  • Submit an answer + script via email

Assessment I (more details)

  • Pass/Fail: A pass will be awarded to a piece submitted by the deadline that demonstrates a thorough and clear effort to engage with and solve the question even if the answers are incorrect or only partially correct.

  • Except for your research project plan due in week 6, your submissions will not be marked individually, but an answer key will be posted on the VLE soon after the deadline.

  • If for any valid reason (illness, etc) you cannot or have not been able submit a piece of work on time, please inform me as soon as possible and I’ll provide an alternative arrangement.

Assessment II (90% of final mark)

Produce a 2,500-word written report based on the quantitative analysis of an available secondary dataset of your choice.

  • Produce a 2,500 word written report based on the quantitative analysis of an available secondary dataset of your choice. This report must address a clearly defined and theoretically informed research question using relevant exploratory and statistical modelling techniques. The report must also include an appendix (not part of the word count) You are also required to submit the annotated code that produced the outputs included in the report.

Assessment II (more details)

  • Consider this a reduced form dissertation in both structure and content

  • You are encouraged to choose research questions that are close to your dissertation project and/or that use the same dataset.

  • More details available the assessment guide/template.

  • Topics: ethnicity and voting behaviour, newspaper readership and attitudes to same-sex marriage, graduate premium, social class and cultural consumption, authoritarian values and populist right vote in India, Welsh language and national identity, etc…

Expectations

  • Engagement from week 1 to 11 with little margin to switch off - PLEASE ATTEND + DO THE HOMEWORK!;
  • Leaving your comfort zone and developing new skills - including a different way of thinking about your discipline;
  • Thinking clearly and systematically about what you are doing or want to do before you do it
  • Making lots of of mistakes - so you need to allow yourselves enough TIME to work!
  • Asking questions if you get stuck or something is still not clear, we are here to help you!

Practice, practice, practice…

This module requires you to practice a lot!

  • There’s homework for key learning units, and if you do the tasks the chances that you will learn the material are much much higher;
  • Over the years I’ve been teaching QSR, the students that have done all the weekly homework have invariably done well, while those who didn’t, did struggle so it’s up to you!
